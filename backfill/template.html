Method

Every detection comes from NASA FIRMS (MODIS C6.1 and VIIRS 375 m C2, on Terra, Aqua, Suomi-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21). Counts are aggregated per calendar month, per platform, day and night apart.

We fit a Poisson panel with three separate effects: year-month (the real fire activity), platform (sensor sensitivity and footprint), and a per-platform slope in local solar time (the orbital drift). The reference is Aqua afternoon, so the published series is MODIS-equivalent and the pre-2012 decade is placed on the same scale as the VIIRS era.

The drift slope is identified because Terra and Aqua slide in opposite directions after 2022 while NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 hold their overpass time. That fixed platform anchors the year-month effect, which is what lets us separate "the fire changed" from "the satellite changed".

Science-quality (SP) rows fit the model. Near-real-time rows are only used for the most recent months and are flagged nrt in every export.

Limitations

  • No cloud-cover correction yet: a cloudy month looks like a quiet month.
  • South-east Australia is poorly reconstructed. The 2019-20 Black Summer is outside the calibration distribution.
  • Chilecito is sparse (about 29 thousand detections in 26 years), so monthly values carry wide intervals.
  • Northern California shows no detectable diurnal slope; large wildfires burn through the overpass window.
  • The MODIS-denial test trains before 2018, so it cannot exercise the drift correction itself.
  • Detections are pixel centres, not ignition points, and counts are not burned area.
