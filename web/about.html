The measurement problem

A different instrument
changes the view.

MODIS and VIIRS have different spatial footprints, sensitivity and acquisition times. Comparing their native detection counts directly can confound a change in reported activity with a change in what was detectable.

Ember Atlas puts both products on the same grid and UTC-day basis, then evaluates a transparent adjustment against continuing MODIS observations.

  1. 01 ObserveReal FIRMS records, source metadata and retrieval coverage.
  2. 02 CompareCommon spatial support and training-only adjustment.
  3. 03 InspectSeparate holdout evidence, remaining error and matching exports.

The scientific contribution

Follow every value
back to evidence.

A source-labeled calendar connects geography, seasonal context and downloadable observations. Eight fixed study areas expose their actual source products and dates.

The Pantanal replay demonstrates a hypothetical source change, with observed MODIS retained as a separate benchmark. Agreement with that reference does not establish ground-truth accuracy. Source-version continuity and usable observation coverage remain unverified.

Behind the project

Created by Diego Shimohama.

Ember Atlas is an individual project developed for the NASA Space Apps Challenge, “Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots.”

The project brings scientific provenance, geographic exploration and reproducible exports into one public workflow. The source repository contains the implementation and recorded verification evidence.

No institutional partnership or endorsement is claimed. NASA FIRMS and the other credited organizations supply resources used by the project.

Open, inspectable evidence

Versioned observations, source manifests, transparent adjustment rules and separate evaluation periods support the published results. MODIS is a comparison reference; agreement is not ground-truth accuracy.
